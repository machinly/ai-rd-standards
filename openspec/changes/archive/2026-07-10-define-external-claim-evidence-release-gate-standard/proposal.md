# Proposal: Define External Claim Evidence Release Gate Standard

## Intent

Add stage 46: a one-person-company standard for external claim, commitment, and evidence release gates across marketing, product UI, docs, developer surfaces, privacy/security pages, support templates, contracts, AI disclosures, and SLA/commercial claims.

## Scope

- Define required `claim-control/` artifacts for production targets that publish objective or customer-reliant claims.
- Cover surface inventory, claim-evidence mapping, release gates, correction runbooks, and periodic claim reviews.
- Connect stages 25, 35, 40, 41, 43, and 45 so external statements align with trust commitments, evidence, data lifecycle, vendor processing, contracts, and developer docs.
- Create a reusable Codex skill and local verifier.

## Out of Scope

- Formal legal advice, advertising clearance, regulatory filings, litigation hold, or customer contract interpretation.
- Replacing trust policy, audit evidence, data lifecycle, vendor, commercial contract, or developer documentation artifacts.
- Full GRC/CLM/CRM/marketing approval platforms.

## Sources

- The Mythical Man-Month and small project management.
- FTC Advertising Substantiation, Advertising and Marketing Basics, and Operation AI Comply.
- NIST AI RMF / Generative AI Profile and NIST Privacy Framework.
- OECD AI Principles.
- Google SRE SLO/SLA guidance.
- OpenAI Data Controls, Usage Policies, and Data Processing Addendum.
- Vercel design references for clear, restrained, user-facing trust and claim surfaces.

## Human Attention

Keep human judgment only for:

- high-risk claims about AI capability, accuracy, professional substitution, security, privacy, data retention, no training, data residency, compliance, SLA, billing/refund, IP, human review, or support response;
- expanding scope from draft/beta/internal/experimental to public/stable/customer-contract;
- accepting unsupported, expired, conflicting, or supplier-dependent evidence;
- customer/developer/auditor/regulator notice or correction;
- recognizing that a contract, SLA, DPA, privacy policy, security page, sales email, support reply, or docs page has created an obligation;
- deciding whether legal, privacy, security, advertising, or contract review is required.
